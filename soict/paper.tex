\begin{abstract}
Prompt compression is reported as a ratio: the fraction of the context a
compressor keeps. We show that this number is not comparable across languages,
and that the gap is large enough to reverse which method is the cheaper one.
Measuring token fertility on parallel WMT24++ sentences across four writing
systems, a context costs $4.97\times$ more tokens in Hindi, $3.89\times$ in Thai
and $2.49\times$ in Vietnamese than its English counterpart under the commercial
tokenisers that bill, so a compressor reporting the same nominal ratio in two
languages is not delivering the same savings. We propose an \emph{effective
ratio} that prices retention in English-equivalent tokens, and show it separates
tokenisers that nominal ratios rank as identical ($1.02\times$ spread nominal,
$1.80\times$ effective). To ground this, we reimplement IterCOMP, a recent
training-free compressor evaluated only on English, and give it its first
evaluation on Vietnamese multi-hop QA - the configuration we release as
IterCOMP-X - using the complete VimQA benchmark alongside MuSiQue,
2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA ($n\ge1{,}003$ each), with six further compression
baselines on a 200-question VimQA subset. Two further effects appear only outside
English: a boolean-answer normalisation step is worth $+8.67$ F1 in Vietnamese
and exactly $0.0$ in English, and non-diacritic text - ubiquitous online -
degrades every branch by about 20 F1, with no measurable extra loss to the
compressed branch.

\keywords{Prompt compression \and Multi-hop question answering \and
Tokeniser fertility \and Vietnamese NLP \and Retrieval-augmented generation}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

retrieval-augmented generation \cite{lewis2020rag} supplies those documents at
two costs: input length grows with their number, and irrelevant passages degrade
reader accuracy.

Prompt compression addresses both, but existing methods
\cite{jiang2023llmlingua,li2023selectivecontext,pan2024llmlingua2,xu2024recomp}
compress \emph{once}, conditioned on the original question, and so miss what
defines multi-hop QA: the information needed at a later hop becomes identifiable
only once the earlier hop's evidence is in. IterCOMP \cite{yun2026itercomp}
embeds that reasoning into the compression loop, but ships no implementation and
is evaluated only on English. Four findings follow, three specific to
Vietnamese:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{An effective ratio} that prices retention in English-equivalent
  tokens. Nominal ratios understate what a Vietnamese context actually costs by
  $2.39\times$, because the tokeniser that bills is not the one that counts.
\item \textbf{A boolean-answer normalisation step} worth $+8.67$ F1 on
  Vietnamese (95\% CI $[+6.98,+10.47]$) and exactly $0.0$ on all three English
  datasets - a linguistic fix, not a metric-inflating one.
\item \textbf{Vietnamese does not handicap the compressor.}
  IterCOMP-X recovers 84\% of the gold-evidence reference on VimQA, more than on any of
  the three English datasets (56--76\%), so the Vietnamese setting is the
  easiest of the four rather than the hardest
  (Section~\ref{sec:vnfindings}).
\item \textbf{The first measurement of non-diacritic Vietnamese.} Stripping
  diacritics costs about 20 F1, and costs gold and compressed context
  indistinguishable amounts ($20.70$ against $18.66$, difference-in-differences
  CI $[-2.71,+6.79]$), so we find no extraction error the compressor adds
  (Table~\ref{tab:undia}).
\end{itemize}

We call this configuration IterCOMP-X and stress that it is \emph{not} a new
algorithm: the loop is unchanged. It is a deployment configuration plus a
metric-level fix, and we report which parts survive significance testing. None of
it is measurable without a working reimplementation; absent a public one, we
rebuild it from the paper's description and redesign every prompt.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Prompt Compression.} Surveys divide the field in two
\cite{li2025promptcompression}. \emph{Extractive} methods keep a subset of
tokens or sentences: LLMLingua \cite{jiang2023llmlingua} drops tokens by
perplexity; LongLLMLingua \cite{jiang2024longllmlingua} adds query awareness;
LLMLingua-2 \cite{pan2024llmlingua2} recasts this as token classification,
faster and \emph{multilingual} - the decisive property here; Selective-Context
\cite{li2023selectivecontext} removes redundant lexical units. \emph{Abstractive}
methods regenerate shorter content, notably RECOMP \cite{xu2024recomp}. All
compress in a single pass.

\paragraph{Iterative Retrieval.} Self-Ask \cite{press2023selfask} and IRCoT
\cite{trivedi2023ircot} decompose questions and interleave retrieval with
reasoning; IterCOMP instead \emph{narrows} the context rather than expanding it.
Every one of these is reported on English benchmarks.

\paragraph{Tokeniser Disparity.} Subword tokenisers split non-English text more
finely, so the same content costs more tokens: Ahia et al.\
\cite{ahia2023allcost} price this against commercial APIs and Petrov et al.\
\cite{petrov2023tokenizers} frame it as unfairness between languages. That
literature measures the cost of \emph{text}. Cross-lingual audits of compression
have since controlled for the disparity \emph{inside} their protocol, matching
budgets in the target tokeniser \cite{lukauskas2026lost}. What is not yet
proposed is a \emph{reported} quantity that carries the correction, so that
published ratios stay comparable across languages - the question
Section~\ref{sec:fertility} takes up.

\paragraph{Vietnamese NLP.} The language is well served at the model level -
the encoder PhoBERT \cite{nguyen2020phobert} and the text-to-text ViT5
\cite{phan2022vit5} are both established - and VimQA \cite{le2022vimqa} gives it a
multi-hop benchmark built to
the HotpotQA format. Compression has begun to reach the language: XProvence
\cite{mohamed2026xprovence} prunes context in 100+ languages and lists Vietnamese
among its transfer targets on MKQA. But that is single-hop, and reports no
per-language retention. To our knowledge no compressor has been evaluated on
Vietnamese \emph{multi-hop} QA, where sentence-level supporting facts make an
Oracle branch available, and none reports what a Vietnamese context costs in the
tokeniser that bills.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Tokeniser Fertility and the Effective Ratio}
\label{sec:fertility}

A compression ratio is reported as a fraction of the context kept, and read as a
saving. That reading assumes the unit being counted costs the same everywhere.
It does not: the tokeniser that bills splits some writing systems far more
finely than English, so the same nominal ratio buys different savings in
different languages. We measure the gap here and propose a correction.

\subsubsection{Cross-lingual Measurement.}
\label{sec:multiling}
Fertility on Vietnamese alone proves nothing: if every language were billed
alike, the quantity would not be worth reporting. Measuring 400 parallel WMT24++
pairs per language shows it is not.

Every language exceeds $1.0$ with non-overlapping intervals
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fertility}), so the mismatch is systematic rather than a Vietnamese quirk; Vietnamese at $2.49\times$ is the \emph{mild} case: Hindi is penalised twice as heavily. The $2.49\times$ here (400 pairs) and the $2.39\times$ in Table~\ref{tab:fertility} (930 pairs) are different samples of the same corpus.



Crucially, fertility depends on which tokeniser is measured. Compression ratios
count with \texttt{\textbackslash w+}, where fertility is only $1.34\times$, so
comparing them across languages is close to fair. API cost is not: it is billed
on the served model's tokeniser, $2.39\times$ under \texttt{cl100k\_base} against
$1.49\times$ under \texttt{o200k\_base}, while bge-m3's XLM-R is $1.19\times$. On
the 200 VimQA contexts here a context averages 1{,}110 whitespace-delimited
syllables but 2{,}364 \texttt{cl100k\_base} tokens, so our ratios
\emph{understate} billing $2\times$.

\subsubsection{Definition.}
\label{sec:effratio}

Write $|x|_{\mathcal{T}}$ for the number of tokens the billed tokeniser
$\mathcal{T}$ assigns to a string $x$, and let $D$ be the retrieved documents and
$P_{\text{comp}}$ the compressed prompt. The quantity compression papers report is

\begin{equation}
\text{Ratio}_{\text{nom}} \;=\; \frac{|P_{\text{comp}}|_{\mathcal{T}}}{|D|_{\mathcal{T}}} .
\end{equation}

Let $\phi_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{L})$ be the fertility of language $\mathcal{L}$
under $\mathcal{T}$, measured over a parallel corpus
$\mathcal{D}_{\text{par}}$ of aligned pairs as the mean of the per-pair ratios

\begin{equation}
\phi_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{L}) \;=\;
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\text{par}}|}
\sum_{(x_{\mathcal{L}}, x_{\text{en}}) \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{par}}}
\frac{|x_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathcal{T}}}{|x_{\text{en}}|_{\mathcal{T}}} ,
\label{eq:fert}
\end{equation}

which is what Table~\ref{tab:fertility} and Fig.~\ref{fig:fertility}
report. We define the \emph{effective ratio} as what the compressed context
costs against an English original of the same content,

\begin{equation}
\text{Ratio}_{\text{eff}} \;=\;
\text{Ratio}_{\text{nom}} \cdot \phi_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{L}) ,
\label{eq:effratio}
\end{equation}

which leaves English unchanged ($\phi = 1$) and rises exactly as far as the
tokeniser penalises the language: keeping a fifth of a Vietnamese context is not
the saving that keeping a fifth of an English one is. A single factor suffices
because fertility belongs to the language-tokeniser pair, not to the compressor,
so reporting both ratios costs one column and leaves the familiar number
intact.

\subsubsection{How Much a Nominal Ratio Conceals.}
The correction is a multiplication, so the effective ratio inherits the fertility
spread by construction; what the following measures is the size of what a
reported ratio hides. We hold the compressed contexts fixed

changed (Table~\ref{tab:effspread}):

\subsection{Iterative Multi-hop Compression}
\label{sec:method}


Given a question $q$ and document set $D$, we seek a compressed context
$P_{\text{comp}}$ with $|P_{\text{comp}}| \ll |D|$ such that the reader still
answers correctly. IterCOMP is training-free: all parameters stay frozen.
Each document is decomposed into sentence-level evidence segments $e_{i,j}$.
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} traces one question through two iterations.


\subsubsection{Evidence Filtering.}
The \emph{semantic} signal is the inner product of dense representations from a
text encoder $E(\cdot)$:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{sem}}(q, e_{i,j}) = E(q)^\top E(e_{i,j}).
\end{equation}
This can miss exact lexical cues. Following M3-Embedding
\cite{chen2024bgem3}, the importance weight of token $t$ in a sequence
$u \in \{q, e_{i,j}\}$ is
\begin{equation}
w_t^{u} = \mathrm{ReLU}(\mathbf{w}_{\text{lex}}^\top E_t(u)),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}_{\text{lex}}$ is a projection vector and $E_t(u)$ the encoder's
contextual state at $t$. The \emph{lexical} score is a sparse
inner product over co-occurring tokens:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{lex}}(q, e_{i,j}) = \sum_{t \in q \cap e_{i,j}} w_t^{q} \cdot w_t^{e_{i,j}}.
\end{equation}
The two are combined into a dual-aspect relevance score:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{dual}} = \lambda \, S_{\text{sem}} + (1-\lambda) \, S_{\text{lex}}.
\label{eq:dual}
\end{equation}
Filtering uses a percentile threshold rather than a fixed top-$k$. Writing
$\mathcal{E} = \{ e_{i,j} \}$ for the segments of every document in $D$ and
$\mathcal{S} = \{ S_{\text{dual}}(q, e) \mid e \in \mathcal{E} \}$ for their scores,
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{cand}} = \{ e \in \mathcal{E} \mid S_{\text{dual}}(q, e) \ge \mathrm{Percentile}(\mathcal{S}, k) \}.
\label{eq:pct}
\end{equation}
The threshold therefore adapts to each question's score distribution rather than
committing to a fixed budget.

\subsubsection{Answerability Judgement and Query Reformulation.}
These two steps let the query evolve with the evidence state instead of staying
fixed at $q$. In step~2 the reader sees the evidence accumulated so far and is
asked whether it suffices to answer $q$, replying with a single token.
The reply is accepted on a substring test for \texttt{YES}, which separates the
two intended labels but means a malformed reply containing that substring stops
the loop early rather than costing an extra pass. When the evidence is judged insufficient, step~3 asks for one short
follow-up question naming the information still missing, and that follow-up
becomes $q'$ for the next pass. The loop ends at whichever comes first: a
sufficient verdict, \texttt{max\_iter}, or a safety cap that stops the loop once
the accumulated evidence exceeds $60\%$ of the source - without it a reader that
never reports sufficiency can return a ``compressed'' context longer than the
original. Segments accumulate rather than replacing one another: from
$E_{\text{accum}} \leftarrow \emptyset$, each pass $m$ applies
$E_{\text{accum}} \leftarrow E_{\text{accum}} \cup E_{\text{cand}}^{(m)}$, and a
segment already chosen is not offered again. We emit $P_{\text{comp}}$ in
selection order rather than document order; restoring document order is a
plausible improvement we did not test. Neither prompt is published in the original
paper; ours are released in full, in Vietnamese and English.

\subsection{Baselines}
Raw Documents concatenates everything with no compression. Oracle
keeps only the gold supporting sentences. It is the reference condition for
selection quality - not a per-question bound, since Raw still beats it on 7 to
10\% of questions - and a diagnostic: if Oracle is low, the bottleneck is the
reader rather than the compressor. LLMLingua-2
\cite{pan2024llmlingua2} is our main compression baseline, chosen because its
token classifier is natively multilingual and covers Vietnamese, so it meets
Vietnamese on equal terms and can run on all four datasets.

Six more run on VimQA at $n=200$: LLMLingua \cite{jiang2023llmlingua},
LongLLMLingua \cite{jiang2024longllmlingua}, Selective-Context
\cite{li2023selectivecontext}, RECOMP \cite{xu2024recomp} in its extractive and
abstractive forms, and CompAct \cite{yoon2024compact}. Each carries an English-only component, so they widen the
field rather than compare like with like, and stay out of the main table. R2C (Retrieve-Read-Compress),
the original paper's second-strongest baseline, has no public checkpoint.

\subsection{Metrics}
We compute Exact Match and token-level F1 under the standard HotpotQA
normalisation, plus the nominal compression ratio. Tables report F1, the less
brittle of the two on free-form answers; EM follows the same ordering and is in
the released per-question records. Nominal retention is counted in \texttt{\textbackslash w+} units, the unit
the original protocol uses - words in English, syllables in Vietnamese, so the
$1.34\times$ figure is a lower bound on word-level fertility;
Eq.~\ref{eq:effratio} converts it to billed cost. That normalisation is built for English, so for Vietnamese we preserve
diacritics: stripping them collapses distinct syllables and renders EM/F1
meaningless.

\paragraph{Reproduction of the Reported Ordering.} The paper reports
Oracle $>$ IterCOMP $>$ Raw $>$ LLMLingua-2. We reproduce it on VimQA (IterCOMP-X
over Raw, $+4.42$, interval clear) and on MuSiQue ($+4.19$; at $n=500$ the same
margin read $+3.31$ and crossed zero, so tripling the sample decided it); the
order matches on 2Wiki, where $+1.00$ does not separate; and on HotpotQA
\textbf{Raw overtakes IterCOMP-X} by $4.66$ F1, interval clear. The four gaps are
therefore $+4.42$, $+4.19$, $+1.00$ and $-4.66$ F1; Section~\ref{sec:reader}
accounts for the reversal.

At $n=50$ those gaps sat inside the range this design can resolve, so we held the
accuracy claim until the full runs settled it. The interpretation that the margin
grows with task complexity does not survive either: $+15.98$ on VimQA against
$+10.72$ on MuSiQue.

\subsection{Reader Dependence of the Ordering}
\label{sec:reader}

Oracle exceeds Raw on every dataset while reading 5.7\% to 16.4\% of the tokens,
confirming the premise the literature rests on: irrelevant passages \emph{do}
degrade accuracy. The size of that gap decides whether compression can exploit it. Oracle/Raw is 2.12 on MuSiQue, 1.61 on 2Wiki and 1.30 on VimQA, where IterCOMP-X
holds its own, but only 1.21 on HotpotQA, where Raw wins outright. \textbf{The premise is
reader-dependent}: a reader that already copes with distractors leaves compression
little to recover. Replacing the reader with Qwen2.5-0.5B on HotpotQA ($n=500$)
moves IterCOMP-X from $-4.66$ to $+7.66$ F1 against Raw ($37.88$ against
$30.22$), the ordering the original paper reports with its own weaker reader.
The two runs are not a clean control, and we say so: following the original
design, our answerability judge is the reader model, so the smaller model both
reads and compresses, and it compresses far less (retention $0.532$ against
$0.192$). The reversal is therefore \emph{consistent with} a reader effect but
does not isolate one; separating them needs a run with the judge pinned at 7B
while the reader shrinks, which we did not have the budget for.

\subsection{Vietnamese-specific Results}
\label{sec:vnfindings}
Four results appear only on Vietnamese. Boolean answer normalisation is worth $+8.67$ F1 on VimQA at $n=1{,}003$, interval
$[+6.98,+10.47]$, and exactly $0.0$ on each of MuSiQue, 2Wiki and HotpotQA,
isolating a language-specific fix from a metric-inflating
one: a reader asked a Vietnamese question often answers ``Yes'', which is
correct and scores $0$ against the gold ``đúng''. The step post-processes the reader's output and is gated by
the \emph{question} - it fires on the closing \emph{phải không} / \emph{đúng
không} frame, never on the gold answer, so it is available at inference and
cannot turn a wrong answer right: a wrong-polarity prediction maps to the
opposite label and still scores zero. Vietnamese does not handicap the compressor:
IterCOMP-X recovers $84\%$ of the gold-evidence reference on VimQA ($51.29$
against $61.03$), the highest of the four, against $76\%$ on HotpotQA, $64\%$ on
2Wiki and $56\%$ on MuSiQue. The Vietnamese dataset is the easiest to recover on,
not the hardest. We do not claim to explain the spread: MuSiQue's twenty
candidate passages against ten elsewhere fit its position at the bottom, but the
three ten-passage datasets themselves range over twenty points.

\paragraph{Diacritic Removal.} Vietnamese is often typed without diacritics
online, a setting no compression paper evaluates, and stripping them is
expensive. Diacritics carry tone (\emph{ma}, \emph{mà}, \emph{má}, \emph{mả},
\emph{mã}, \emph{mạ} collapse to one form) and segmental contrasts (ă â ê ô ơ ư,
đ), so \emph{đang} and \emph{dáng} merge too; across VimQA this shrinks the
syllable-type inventory by $21.4\%$, and both branches lose about 20 F1. Gold context leads throughout, by $10.52$ F1 with
diacritics and by $8.48$ and $6.75$ without, every interval clear of zero at
$n=500$ where none separated at $n=200$. What stripping does \emph{not} do is
single out the compressor: gold loses $20.70$ F1 and the compressed branch
$18.66$, a difference-in-differences of $+2.04$, CI $[-2.71,+6.79]$. We attribute the loss to the reader failing on merged
syllables, though the interval leaves room for a small effect either way.

One point matters for anyone repeating this: supporting facts address
documents by title, so diacritics must come off the annotations as well as the
context. Strip one side only and the match breaks, leaving the gold branch
empty.

\subsection{Error Analysis}
\label{sec:vietnamese}
\label{sec:erroranalysis}
\label{app:errors}

We hand-categorised 50 IterCOMP-X predictions on VimQA. Most errors belong to the
\emph{reader}: Oracle sees the gold evidence yet reaches only 61.03 F1, so
\textbf{80\%} of IterCOMP-X's 48.7 F1 of error survives perfect retrieval. One
error in ten is not a compression failure at all: a yes/no question answered
correctly in substance but in the wrong surface form, scoring F1 of 0. A smaller reader does this far more often on the identical
prompt - ``tức là yes'' for ``đúng'', the language confusion small models are
prone to \cite{marchisio2024confusion} - so boolean answers need language-aware
normalisation rather than a compression fix. The rest give a wrong but related entity inside a correctly
retained segment, or are compositional, where the Raw-Oracle gap is widest.

A further \textbf{9.8\% of VimQA is a single question template} - \emph{``X lớn
tuổi hơn Y phải không?''}, ``is $X$ older than $Y$?'', and its negation
\emph{nhỏ tuổi hơn} - on which 44.9\% of answers score zero against 37.1\% elsewhere,
because the task is arithmetic over two birth dates rather than retrieval; under
a 0.5B reader it collapses to 92.1\% zeros.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

A compression ratio is not comparable across languages, and a nominal figure
conceals a $1.80\times$ spread in true cost across tokenisers it reports as
identical to within $1.02\times$; the effective ratio is one extra column and
fixes that. Reproducing IterCOMP without its code and giving it its first
Vietnamese evaluation, released as IterCOMP-X, shows sentence-level beating
token-level compression on all four datasets while winning on three of four
against the uncompressed baseline. Neither Vietnamese nor the loss of its
diacritics handicaps the compressor; the reader does, failing on ${\sim}30\%$ of
questions even given gold evidence.

Three limits bound these claims, and each names its own next step. Every headline
number comes from one reader, and the check on that is confounded, since the
answerability judge shares the reader model - pinning it at 7B while shrinking
only the reader would separate the two effects and settle whether the HotpotQA
reversal, where IterCOMP-X loses $4.66$ F1 to the uncompressed baseline, belongs
to the reader. Fertility is measured on four writing systems while compression
runs on two, so the effective ratio is argued generally and shown on one language
pair; a Hindi or Thai multi-hop set with sentence-level supporting facts would
test it where fertility is worst rather than mildest, and none exists today.
Absolute numbers are not comparable to the original paper's, which released no
prompts and used a different reader at a different operating point (0.340
retention against 0.14).
